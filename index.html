Chapter 1

Introduction

Motivation · research problem · research questions and contributions

Background and motivation

  • Link-level traffic volumes inform infrastructure investment, congestion management, emissions regulation and public-health assessment.
  • Road transport dominates travel. Cars, vans and taxis carried 86% of passenger-kilometres in Great Britain in 2022.
  • Much recent deep learning forecasts traffic minutes to hours ahead from recent sensor observations.
  • Planning faces a different information constraint. Many roads have no measurement history, and traffic must be related to population, employment, land use and network structure.
  • Empirical setting: interurban highway traffic on England's Strategic Road Network (SRN), with socioeconomic inputs for England and Wales.

Three requirements used to assess the models

AccuracyAgreement with held-out traffic counts
Spatial generalisationPrediction in regions excluded from fitting
InterpretabilityExamination of learned demand-related relationships

Related but distinct. Random hold-out accuracy does not establish geographical transfer, and accuracy alone does not establish what learned quantities mean (Figure 1.1).

  • Existing methods address portions of the demand-to-flow chain, or rely on traffic-state observations or intermediate demand quantities when predicting.
  • Less developed setting: link-count supervision, with inference from external socioeconomic, land-use and network information only.
  • How transport information is represented then matters as much as the choice of learning algorithm: flexible representation learning or explicit theory-informed structure.

Central hypothesis. External demand-related characteristics and network structure contain sufficient information to support useful link-level traffic estimation without traffic-state measurements at prediction time, and transport theory can guide the representation of these relationships in ways that support both prediction and interpretation.

Explicit, inspectable structure

  • Gravity distribution $T_{ij}=A_iO_iB_jD_j\,f(c_{ij})$. Wilson's entropy derivation gives $f(c_{ij})=\exp(-\beta c_{ij})$.
  • Logit mode choice. User-equilibrium or stochastic user-equilibrium assignment with volume–delay functions.
  • Each intermediate quantity can be compared with its own observations.

Calibration is not validation

  • OD matrices are adjusted until assigned flows reproduce counts. Agreement with the same counts measures in-sample calibration.
  • Many OD and route-flow patterns reproduce the same link counts.
  • Local fit is absorbed into a location-specific OD matrix, which cannot normally be transferred. More transferable parameters may be too coarse for link-level flows without local information.

Wherever a model is used beyond the network it was calibrated on, it needs validation in places whose counts did not contribute to fitting (Section 2.2).

Case study: forecasting with ST-MetaNet

  • ST-MetaNet on 498 monitored segments of England's trunk-road network. Speed and volume at 15-minute intervals, twelve steps in and twelve out, chronological 70/10/20 split.
  • Blind extrapolation. Forecasts continue the recent trend smoothly and miss turning points.
  • Error accumulation. In recursive decoding an early error is carried forward and grows over the horizon.
  • Lag. A one-step variant (30-minute intervals, six hours of history) follows the daily profile but reproduces abrupt changes only after they enter the input history.

The forecast is conditional on an observed traffic state. Estimating typical volumes where no traffic history exists is a different task.

Evaluation design and metrics

Random CV (five folds)

Held-out target edges spread across the network. Mukara keeps every year of an edge in the same fold. DeepDemand splits target edges 70/10/20 into training, validation and test sets.

Spatial CV (nine folds)

Each official region of England is held out in turn. The test region contributes no traffic counts to fitting. DeepDemand draws its validation set from the other eight regions.

  • All models in a study share the same partitions. Results are means (standard deviations) across folds.
  • Both schemes test spatial estimation. Neither tests future-year forecasting or the response to an intervention.

Metrics

$$\mathrm{MAE}=\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i\lvert y_i-\hat{y}_i\rvert\quad\text{(vehicles per day)}$$ $$R^2=1-\frac{\sum_i(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_i(y_i-\bar{y})^2}$$ $$\mathrm{wMAPE}=100\,\frac{\sum_i\lvert y_i-\hat{y}_i\rvert}{\sum_i y_i}$$ $$\mathrm{GEH}_i=\sqrt{\frac{2(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2}{y_i+\hat{y}_i}},\qquad \mathrm{MGEH}=\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i\mathrm{GEH}_i$$

GEH depends on the flow period. The usual references of 5 and 10 apply to hourly flows (Backup).

Mukara

RQ1 · Can highway traffic be estimated from external socioeconomic characteristics and road-network structure without using traffic observations as prediction inputs?

Mukara: objectives and data

First test of the prediction setting, with deliberately limited theory structure: no OD pairs, OD matrix or route assignment. The name derives from the Japanese for “from nothing”, as no sensor history is an input.

Objectives

  1. Estimate national highway traffic from socioeconomic characteristics and network structure.
  2. Evaluate accuracy and spatial generalisation against baselines.
  3. Examine the contributions of spatial context, network propagation and feature groups.

Data

  • Trunk-road graph: 181 junction nodes, 498 directed edges (249 connections in both directions).
  • One representative TRIS sensor per edge, chosen from 3,984 candidates by topology and data availability.
  • 1 km grids: population and employment for 2015–2022, plus four land-use, three road-level and five POI channels.
  • Five Google Routes API edge features: duration, distance, straight-line distance, speed, detour factor.
  • Target: mean weekday daily traffic per edge and year (8 × 498 edge–year observations).

Architecture

Test MGEH and MAE by GAT depth (Figure 4.12)

  • Edge features alone carry limited information. Endpoint embeddings add local context.
  • Full graph propagation improves further. Five or six GAT layers perform best, and deeper models deteriorate.
  • Ridge and random forest under spatial CV perform like the variant without GAT layers, so the gain is consistent with multi-hop propagation.

Feature groups

From Mukara to DeepDemand

Scope of the Mukara result

  • Predictive, not causal. The feature analyses measure predictive contribution only.
  • 498 monitored segments. Repeated years add examples but not locations.
  • One 2023 OSM snapshot serves all study years.
  • Transfer tested within England only. No temporal extrapolation, congestion or mode-shift response.
  • A heteroscedastic Gaussian extension trained less stably and raised MAE from about 9,000 to about 12,000 vehicles per day, so point prediction is kept.

DeepDemand

RQ2 · Can a more explicit representation of OD relationships informed by travel-demand theory support prediction and interpretation at a finer road-segment resolution?

DeepDemand: theory-informed design

Two methodological developments:

  1. Network preprocessing. A competitive two-source Dijkstra search extracts origin and destination regions for each target edge. Shortest-path screening keeps the OD pairs whose shortest route uses the edge.
  2. Differentiable model. Learns a non-negative OD interaction score and a travel-time attenuation for every screened pair, and sums their contributions into a link volume.

Trained end to end on observed link volumes. No observed OD flows are used, so the learned OD scores are model-derived quantities, not trip counts.

Network, features and targets

  • Driving network. OpenStreetMap Great Britain, 1 January 2025. All drivable roads kept, then simplified to about 6.3 million nodes and 7.2 million directed edges. Free-flow travel time from the posted speed limit, or a road-class fallback speed.
  • LSOA features. 121 variables on population, employment, households and car availability, deprivation, land use and POIs, standardised and reduced to 64 principal components. Each LSOA is attached to the node nearest its population-weighted centroid.
  • Targets. TRIS sensors matched within 10 m to motorway, trunk and link edges. Annual AADT for 2017–2024 from sensor-years with at least 200 valid observations. Median across sensors, then across years.
  • 5,088 target edges. Mean AADT 25,243, standard deviation 18,893, maximum 113,436 vehicles per day.

Observed AADT: map, distributions by road type and day-to-day variability (Figure 5.5)

Target-specific OD extraction and screening

(a) Competitive expansion, shown with a 20-minute cutoff. (b) A retained pair. (c) An excluded pair (Figure 5.7)

  • Competitive two-source Dijkstra. From $e^\star=(u,v)$, search backward from $u$ for origins and forward from $v$ for destinations with one shared priority queue. The first search to reach a node claims it, so $\mathcal{O}_{e^\star}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{e^\star}$ do not overlap.
  • Cutoff $\tau_{\max}=60$ min on each side. In the NTS 2017–2024 (985,676 car trips), 3.7% of weighted car trips exceed 60 minutes.
  • Screening. Keep $(o,d)$ only if a shortest route passes through the edge: $$\tau_{\mathrm{O}}(o)+\tau_{e^\star}+\tau_{\mathrm{D}}(d)=\tau_{\mathrm{OD}}(o,d)$$
  • National scale. Compressed sparse row network (about 187 MB), compiled Dijkstra kernels, cached shortest-path trees and parallel workers. All 5,088 edges in about 33 minutes, 1.64 billion screened pairs across target edges.

Differentiable demand-to-flow model

Figure 5.8

$$\mathbf{h}_o=f_{\mathrm{O}}\big(\mathbf{x}^{(v)}_o\big),\qquad \mathbf{h}_d=f_{\mathrm{D}}\big(\mathbf{x}^{(v)}_d\big)$$ $$s_{od}=\mathrm{Softplus}\big(f_{\mathrm{OD}}([\mathbf{h}_o\Vert\mathbf{h}_d])\big)\ \ge 0$$ $$p_{od}=\sigma\big(f_{\mathrm{t}}(\tilde{\tau}_{od})\big)\in(0,1),\qquad \tilde{\tau}_{od}=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{OD}}(o,d)-\bar{\tau}}{s_{\tau}}$$ $$S_{e^\star}=\sum_{(o,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{e^\star}}s_{od}\,p_{od},\qquad \hat{y}_{e^\star}=\gamma\sqrt{S_{e^\star}}$$
  • $f_{\mathrm{O}}$, $f_{\mathrm{D}}$, $f_{\mathrm{OD}}$ and $f_{\mathrm{t}}$ are small MLPs trained jointly. $\bar{\tau}=3600$ s, $s_{\tau}=1000$ s, $\gamma=100$.
  • Each pair contributes independently. There is no softmax over pairs.
  • Mean squared error loss. AdamW with staged learning rates $3\times10^{-3}$, $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$, early stopping on validation MGEH.

Interpretation: learned travel-time attenuation

Attenuation $p_{od}$ against OD travel time: mean over random-CV folds and range across folds (Figure 5.13)

  • Nearly flat below about 15 minutes, then falls from about 0.97 to 0.09 between 15 and 45 minutes.
  • Plateaus near 0.05 beyond 45 to 50 minutes and rises slightly to about 0.07 at 90 to 120 minutes.
  • An MLP outperformed linear-logit and exponential-decay forms for $f_{\mathrm{t}}$.
  • Narrow range across folds. Spatial-CV curves share the shape, with modest shifts between held-out regions (Backup).
  • Estimated from link counts only. It attenuates latent OD scores and is not an independently measured behavioural response.

Interpretation: area characteristics and OD scores

(a) Feature domains at origin (O) and destination (D). Red: high feature value (Figure 5.15)

(g) Household car availability

  • Random-forest surrogates, one per feature group, fitted to $\log s_{od}$ for 50,000 pairs sampled from 107,317,306 unique screened pairs. SHAP values on the raw LSOA variables.

Interpretation: origin and destination potentials

  • Destination potential of an LSOA: its mean OD score when acting as destination, per unit area. Origin potential is defined in the same way.
  • Destination potential is polycentric. The City of London, Canary Wharf, the Thames corridor, Heathrow, Gatwick and Manchester Airport stand out. Birmingham Airport is weaker.
  • Origin potential is more diffuse and extends into suburban and peri-urban areas.
  • The model saw no OD flows and no generation or attraction totals. The maps are hypotheses to test against such data, not identified OD estimates.

DeepDemand: scope and limitations

Mode choice

Not represented. Car availability and accessibility carry some modal information, but modal shares and competing services are not modelled.

Static free-flow travel times

Extraction and screening ignore time of day, recurrent congestion and incidents. This suits a long-term AADT target but not time-varying route choice.

Assignment-like mapping

Screened contributions are summed. There is no equilibrium, capacity or congestion response, and equally short alternative routes are not split.

Spatial estimation only

No short-term variability and no predictive uncertainty. Temporal forecasting needs its own validation design.

Surrogate interpretation

SHAP describes associations between raw LSOA variables and fitted OD scores, not the model's internal use of principal components.

Freight beyond one hour

The NTS covers personal travel only, and mean HGV length of haul is about 105 km. Cars are about 74% of vehicle miles and HGVs about 6%, so the catchment is supported for cars but not for freight.

Answers to the research questions

RQ1
Yes, within the study setting. Mukara estimates weekday traffic on 498 trunk-road segments from external characteristics and network structure, with test $R^2$ 0.583 under random CV and 0.521 under spatial CV, ahead of the gravity, ridge and random-forest baselines. Spatial context and network propagation both contribute.
RQ2
Yes, for specific structural choices. DeepDemand reaches test $R^2$ 0.706 and 0.647 on 5,088 edges, with the lowest MAE and wMAPE of all models under its protocol. OD extraction and screening lift the same tree learners far above their pooled versions. Ablations support partitioning, screening, learned attenuation and sublinear aggregation, but not separate encoders.
RQ3
Both models transfer across English regions (0.583 to 0.521, and 0.706 to 0.647), while high-capacity trees lose more. DeepDemand exposes a stable attenuation curve, feature associations and spatial potentials. These are model-derived quantities that suggest hypotheses, not identified behavioural parameters.

Contributions

1 · Observation-supervised external-input estimation

Two models learn link-level traffic from observed counts, with only external socioeconomic and network information at prediction: no traffic-state input and no observed OD matrix. The claim is this conjunction at national scale, not the first use of external variables.

2 · Explicit test of geographical generalisation

Region-held-out CV alongside random CV in both studies. It separates models that transfer from models that only fit, and it bounds the claims: it is not future-year forecasting, transfer to another country or intervention analysis.

3 · Theory-informed OD representation at national scale

Competitive two-source Dijkstra, shortest-path screening, learned interaction and travel-time attenuation. Routing on 6.3 million nodes becomes a reusable preprocessing step, and the representation is separable from the neural architecture.

Observed link counts can supervise models that learn demand-to-flow relationships from external characteristics and network structure. How that relationship is represented matters for accuracy, transfer and interpretation, beyond model capacity alone.

Planning relevance and scope

Supports

  • Estimates at unmonitored roads where compatible socioeconomic and network data exist.
  • Network screening, preliminary demand assessment and targeting of new monitoring.
  • Conditional scenario exploration under stated assumptions, such as the 2040 population projections.
  • Exploratory analysis of learned demand structure: attenuation, OD scores and potentials.

Does not establish

  • Validated long-term traffic forecasts.
  • Causal effects of housing, pricing or infrastructure interventions.
  • Mode choice, congested equilibrium or time-varying route choice.
  • Transfer to other countries, dense urban networks or different road hierarchies.
  • Precise engineering estimates without local calibration or measurement.

Traffic observations still matter. Performance depends on the representativeness and quality of the training counts, and a new setting needs validation and a check that it lies within the training domain.

Directions for further work

Coverage and inputs

  • Automatic sensor matching for more targets
  • Time-varying land use, capacity and junction detail
  • Finer zones, which need faster screening

Demand structure

  • Explicit mode choice
  • Multi-path, equilibrium or dynamic assignment
  • Time-dependent costs and freight-specific catchments
  • Synthetic benchmarks with known ground truth

Architecture and scaling

  • Other learners on the same OD representation
  • Simpler variants such as a shared encoder
  • Faster routing and screening for finer zones

Temporal validation

  • Rolling-origin tests on genuinely later data
  • Joint projections of population, employment and transport supply

Predictive uncertainty

  • Ensembles and Bayesian methods
  • Assessment by calibration, coverage and interval width

Transfer and causality

  • Other countries and few-shot calibration
  • Causal designs for intervention questions

Four-step model: key relationships

Distribution

$$T_{ij}=A_iO_iB_jD_j\,f(c_{ij})$$ $$A_i=\Big[\sum_j B_jD_jf(c_{ij})\Big]^{-1}$$ $$B_j=\Big[\sum_i A_iO_if(c_{ij})\Big]^{-1}$$

Maximising $\log W$ with $W=T!/\prod_{ij}T_{ij}!$, subject to the margins and the total cost $C=\sum_{ij}T_{ij}c_{ij}$, gives $f(c_{ij})=\exp(-\beta c_{ij})$ (Wilson).

Mode choice

$$P^{k}_{ij}=\frac{\exp(V^{k}_{ij})}{\sum_{k'\in\mathcal{K}_{ij}}\exp(V^{k'}_{ij})},\qquad T^{k}_{ij}=P^{k}_{ij}\,T_{ij}$$

Assignment

$$\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ij}}f_{ijr}=q_{ij},\qquad v_a=\sum_{ij}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{ij}}\delta_{a,ijr}\,f_{ijr}$$ $$\min_{\mathbf{f}}\ \sum_a\int_0^{v_a}t_a(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$$
  • User equilibrium: all used routes of an OD pair have equal, minimal cost. Link costs follow a volume–delay function such as BPR.
  • Many OD and route-flow patterns give the same link totals, so counts alone do not identify a unique OD matrix.

Section 2.2

GEH on daily volumes

  • Reference values of 5 and 10 come from hourly assignment validation. Multiplying both flows by $c$ multiplies GEH by $\sqrt{c}$.
  • If daily traffic is about ten times peak-hour traffic, illustrative daily levels are about 16 and 32. They are not acceptance thresholds.
  • DeepDemand: 19.5% of target edges below 16 and 37.0% below 32 under random CV, 18.3% and 36.3% under spatial CV.
  • Mukara: 18% of sensors below 16 and 49% below 32 in the random-CV test sets. Sensor-level MGEH quartiles 23.88, 32.10 and 72.51.
  • Many individual links keep substantial error even where network-level $R^2$, MAE and wMAPE are useful.

Relation to $R^2$. With small relative errors and a volume-weighted RMS GEH $G$,

$$R^2\approx 1-G^2\,\frac{\bar{y}}{\sigma_y^2}$$

For this network $\bar{y}=25{,}243$ and $\sigma_y=18{,}893$, so $G=16$ corresponds to $R^2\approx0.982$ and $G=32$ to $R^2\approx0.928$.

RMS GEH is not MGEH, so there is no general conversion from the reported MGEH to $R^2$ (Appendix A.3).

DeepDemand: baseline construction

  • OD-structured features (242): mean raw LSOA vector over the unique screened origins, concatenated with the mean over the unique screened destinations.
  • Pooled features (121): mean over all LSOAs in the unscreened one-hour region, with no origin–destination separation and no screening.
  • Gravity: $G_{e^\star}(\lambda)=\sum_{(o,d)\in\mathcal{P}_{e^\star}}M_oM_d\exp(-\lambda\,\tau_{\mathrm{OD}}(o,d))$, with masses from population plus employment and $\log y=a_0+a_1\log(G+\epsilon)$. $\lambda$ is chosen on validation data.
  • GAT: two-hub local graph. Candidate origins link to $u$ and destinations to $v$, with access and egress times as edge features, and one to six attention layers chosen on validation data. No screening.
  • Hyperparameters of every model are selected on validation MGEH. Test data are used only for final evaluation.

Spatial-CV attenuation curves against the random-CV mean (Figure 5.13)

DeepDemand: training settings and compute

  • Random CV: 70/10/20 split of target edges in each fold. Spatial CV: one region for testing and 12.5% of the other regions' edges for validation.
  • Edge-centric batches: eight target edges per pass, gradients accumulated over four passes, an effective batch of 32.
  • AdamW, weight decay $10^{-4}$, gradient-norm clipping at 5.
  • Learning rates $3\times10^{-3}$, $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$. A stage ends when validation MGEH fails to improve by 0.1 over 15 evaluations, and its best checkpoint seeds the next stage.
  • MLP widths: $f_{\mathrm{O}}$ and $f_{\mathrm{D}}$ [16, 16], $f_{\mathrm{OD}}$ [16, 8], $f_{\mathrm{t}}$ [16, 16].
  • Workstation: Intel Core i7-13700F, 16 GB RAM, NVIDIA RTX 4060 Ti with 8 GB, PyTorch 2.5.1.
  • Preprocessing of all 5,088 edges: about 33 minutes, done once and cached.
  • Training: about 28 minutes per random-CV fold and 50 minutes per spatial-CV fold.
  • Shortest-path trees are cached to $2\tau_{\max}$. A pair whose full journey through the edge exceeds this horizon can be missed, a small approximation.
  • Data: doi.org/10.17863/CAM.127892. Code: github.com/yueli901/DeepDemand.
